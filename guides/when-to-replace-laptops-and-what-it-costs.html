When to replace laptops and equipment, and what it will cost

Published 2026-11-26. Written by Trackora. The example in this article is fictional.

Equipment does not fail on a schedule you can set, but your budget has to be set in advance. The practical answer is a replacement plan built from three facts for each group of equipment: when it was bought, how long you plan to keep it, and what it would cost to replace today.

Replacement cost is not the original price

A refresh budget pays for new equipment at current prices. The original price is history. In the fictional example, finance laptops bought at 1,100 each would cost 1,350 to replace today, up 23 percent, while 24-inch monitors bought at 210 would cost 190, down about ten percent. Keeping both numbers shows how far your old figures have drifted. Check current prices against a recent quote before you rely on them.

Useful life is a planning assumption

Pick a useful life for each category from your own experience and policy. It is not a manufacturer recommendation and not a guarantee. A reasonable starting point is a category default that you override for items that work harder, such as rugged tablets used in a warehouse. The planned replacement year is simply the purchase year plus the life you choose, unless you already know the date.

Sort items into four states

  • Overdue: the planned year has passed.
  • Due this year: replace now.
  • Approaching: due within your planning window, for example two years.
  • Planned: later.

In the example, 10 of 24 groups are overdue, worth 128,430 at today's prices. That number is the first thing a budget owner should see.

Turn it into an annual budget

Add the overdue spend to the next few years of planned spend and divide by the number of years. With a five-year horizon the example needs about 66,100 a year. If you pay for the overdue items all at once, you have one large year followed by smaller ones, so decide whether to smooth them.

Test the choices that change the answer

Four levers matter: prices, useful life, timing and quantity. Extending laptop life by a year does not reduce the total; it moves cost to later. Deferring all replacements by a year lowers this year's bill and raises next year's. Seeing that on a chart helps a finance partner decide on purpose.

What this is not

This is replacement planning, not accounting. It does not calculate depreciation, and it will not tell you the best economic moment to replace heavy machinery. It is built for IT and office equipment.
