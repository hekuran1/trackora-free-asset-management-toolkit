What to put in an employee equipment policy, and what to leave to your lawyer

Published 2026-11-26. Written by Trackora. The example in this article is fictional.

A policy that nobody signs, and forms that nobody completes, will not get a laptop back. A usable equipment set has a few short documents that work together. This is general guidance, not legal advice. Have your legal or HR team review anything you adopt.

The set

  • Acceptable use policy: who owns the equipment, how it may be used and protected, what to report, and when to return it.
  • Equipment agreement: records what was issued to whom, with acknowledgements.
  • Issue acknowledgment: the short form that goes with every handover.
  • Damage or loss report: facts, evidence and follow-up.
  • Return confirmation: what came back, in what condition, and what is still missing.
  • Remote working policy: storage, transport and return arrangements for equipment at home.
  • A manager guide: how to roll it out and keep records.

What a good acceptable use policy covers

Purpose and scope; that equipment stays the organisation's property; acceptable use and the limits of personal use; physical protection; information security responsibilities; reporting damage or loss quickly; a rule against unauthorised transfers; return obligations; employee and manager responsibilities; and a review date.

Make the forms do the work

At issue, record the asset reference, serial number, accessories and condition. At damage or loss, ask for the incident, where it happened, immediate actions and any data at risk. At return, inspect against the issue record and note what is missing. Offboarding is where most losses start, so connect the return step to the departure process.

What to leave to your lawyer

Whether and how an employee can be asked to pay for loss or damage depends on your contracts and on local law, so it is left blank in the template for your legal or HR team to write. The documents contain no legal citations and do not claim to comply with any law or standard. Use them as a starting point, not as finished legal text.

Keep a record you can find

Whatever you use, a spreadsheet, a shared folder or software, keep the signed issue record, the asset reference, the current holder, any transfer, and the return confirmation together.
