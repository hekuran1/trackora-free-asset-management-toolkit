How to compare supplier quotes fairly before you look at price

Published 2026-11-26. Written by Trackora. The example in this article is fictional.

Three quotes on the same page rarely say the same thing. One includes tax, one adds delivery, one quietly assumes a shorter warranty. Before you rank by price, make the quotes comparable.

Seven checks

  1. Same quantity and specification. If quantities differ, compare cost per unit.
  2. Tax on the same basis. If a price includes tax, divide it out to find the before-tax amount. If it excludes tax, add it at the right rate. Then every supplier shows before-tax, tax and total payable.
  3. Delivery as its own line. Free delivery and a lower unit price can be the same deal.
  4. Discounts applied to goods only. A percentage discount normally does not reduce delivery.
  5. Quote validity. An expired quote is a question, not a price. Flag it.
  6. What is included. Installation, on-site support, training, take-back of old equipment. Record yes, no or unknown for each supplier.
  7. Payment terms. Fifty percent up front is a cost of its own.

A worked example

Five fictional suppliers quote forty laptops. Supplier B quotes 1,520 per unit including tax; the others quote before tax. After normalising, the totals payable are 60,732, 60,800, 58,920, 66,540 and 62,712. The cheapest, 58,920, also has the shortest warranty and a return-to-base support model.

Score what price does not show

Choose criteria and weights before you see the results: price, warranty, delivery time, technical suitability, support and supplier reliability. Weights must total 100 percent. Price, warranty and delivery can be scored from the quotes, for example the lowest price gets full marks and others score in proportion. The rest are judgments you record with a reason.

In the example the highest weighted score, 90 percent, belongs to a supplier that is about 3 percent dearer than the cheapest, because it has the longest warranty and fast delivery. That is a reason to discuss, not to decide automatically.

Record the decision

Write down each supplier's advantages, risks and open questions, then the supplier chosen, why, any conditions before ordering, and who approved it. A decision with its reasoning on file is easier to defend later, and easier to repeat.
